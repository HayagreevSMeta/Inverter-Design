SOURCE 1: TI Whitepaper "Design Priorities in EV Traction Inverters" (SPRAD58B, Sept 2022, Rev Feb 2026)

- Switching frequency: typically 5kHz to 30kHz for two-level inverters

- 800+V technology with increased switching transient voltages (900V cars available as of 2025)
- Three-level inverter offers "significantly higher efficiency and lower harmonic distortion"
- Three-level allows "use of a smaller EMI filter"
- Gate-drive strength adjustment to reduce overshoot and improve efficiency
- Coreless current sensing trending (TMCS2100-Q1)

INDUSTRY vs ACADEMIA DESIGN DIFFERENCES:
Industry focuses on:
1. Power density (kW/L) - DOE target 100kW/L
2. Efficiency at rated AND partial load
3. EMC compliance (CISPR 25)
4. Functional safety (ISO 26262 ASIL D)
5. Thermal cycling reliability (power cycling, mission profiles)
6. Cost optimization ($/kW)
7. Active discharge for safety
8. Gate driver integration and protection
9. DC-link capacitor lifetime (ripple current rating)
10. Busbar design and parasitic inductance

Academia focuses on:
1. Novel modulation strategies
2. THD minimization
3. Topology comparison
4. Control algorithms
5. Simulation validation
6. Steady-state analysis
7. Idealized component models

SOURCE 10: Wolfspeed HM Power Module Family (wolfspeed.com, May 2026)
- Platform: 175°C continuous junction operation, Si3N4 power substrate, AlSiC baseplate
- Low inductance: 4.9 nH (critical for high dv/dt SiC switching)
- Module size: 110 x 6 x 12.2 mm (62mm baseplate compatible)
- Applications: Railway & Traction, Solar & Renewable Energy

Key modules for 800V EV traction (1200V, Half-Bridge):
- CAR600M12HN6: 1200V, 600A, Half-Bridge Rectifier, 175°C
- CLB800M12HM3P: 1200V, 800A, Common-Source, 1.3mΩ, 175°C
- CAB760M12HM3: 1200V, 760A, Half-Bridge, 1.33mΩ, 175°C
- CAS480M12HM3: 1200V, 480A, Half-Bridge, 2.29mΩ, 175°C

For our design (peak ~136A per phase, 3-level so each device sees Vdc/2=400V):
- CAS480M12HM3 is most appropriate: 480A rating gives >3x margin
- Rds_on = 2.29mΩ at 25°C (much lower than our assumed 4.0mΩ)
- This is a half-bridge module - for FC topology need 4 switches per phase

ALTERNATIVE: For 3-level FC, could use individual switch modules or discrete SiC MOSFETs
- Wolfspeed C3M0021120K: 1200V, 21mΩ discrete (paralleled)
- Infineon FF8MR12W2M1_B11: 1200V automotive SiC module

CRITICAL FINDING: Our simulation uses Rds_on = 4.0mΩ which is reasonable for a 
450A-class module. The actual Wolfspeed CAS480M12HM3 has 2.29mΩ at 25°C.
This means our conduction losses may be ~43% too high. However, for a 3-level FC topology,
we need 4 half-bridge positions per phase (not a standard 6-pack), so the module 
selection is more complex.

SOURCE 11: Charged EVs "A closer look at multilevel traction inverters" (March 2025, Jeffrey Jenkins)
KEY INDUSTRY CRITICISMS OF FC MLI FOR EV TRACTION:
1. "fiendishly difficult to control" - FC type acknowledged as hardest to control
2. "introduces the thorny problem of how to pre-charge its flying capacitors during startup"
3. "the capacitors in the voltage dividers have to carry considerable ripple current, 
   both at the fundamental (motor) frequency and the PWM frequency"
4. "just the AC voltage divider capacitors required by most MLI topologies will likely 
   take up as much volume all by themselves as a complete 2-level VSI"
5. "the argument for MLIs in EVs is a tough one to sell" when LC filters on 2-level 
   can achieve similar EMI reduction
6. NPC type is "far easier" to control than FC type
7. FC type advantages: higher fault tolerance, lower ultimate THD achievable
8. For 800V with SiC: "not so relevant with SiC MOSFETs as they are widely available 
   in the 1.2 kV and 1.7 kV ratings"
9. T-type inverter saves switches but requires full-voltage-rated devices

SOURCE 12: TDK Flying Capacitor Solution Guide (Nov 2022)
- FC sizing equation: C_FC = I_peak / (2 * f_sw * ΔV_FC)
- For their example: 60A peak, 16kHz, 80Vpp ripple → 24µF
- Film capacitors preferred for FC: high ripple current, long life (>100k hours)
- CeraLink ceramic alternative for space-constrained designs
- Ambient temperature assumption: 60°C (heat from power modules doesn't significantly affect FC)
- Key selection criteria: resonance frequency, energy density, ripple current, 
  temperature rating (up to 125°C for film)

SOURCE 13: Anderson et al., IEEE TPEL 2020 (162 citations)
"Three levels are not enough: Scaling laws for multilevel converters"
- Provides scaling laws for inductor current ripple and capacitor voltage ripple
- Compares 2-level 400V vs 3-level 800V converters
- Important reference for justifying 3-level at 800V

SOURCE 14: McGrath & Holmes, IEEE TPEL 2011 (173 citations)
"Enhanced voltage balancing of a flying capacitor multilevel converter using PD modulation"
- PD-PWM has superior natural balancing characteristics
- Balancing time constant reduces with higher switching frequency
- Seminal reference for our modulation choice

SOURCE 15: AQG 324 (ECPE Working Group, 2017-present)
- European Qualification Guideline for Power Modules in Power Electronics Converter Units
- Tests: thermal cycling, power cycling, humidity, vibration, mechanical shock
- Referenced standards: IEC 60749, JEDEC JESD22
- VW and other OEMs use this as baseline for supplier qualification
- Key test: Power cycling (Coffin-Manson model): N_f = A * (ΔTj)^(-α) * exp(Ea/(k*Tj_max))
  Typical: α ≈ 4-5 for wire bonds, Ea ≈ 0.4 eV

SOURCE 16: Cougo et al. (2026) "Influence of high frequency power cycles on SiC"
- Thermal cycles at motor current frequency observed in SiC automotive powertrain
- "High frequency" loss models needed for accurate lifetime prediction
- Mission profile approach essential for reliability

SOURCE 17: Chakraborty et al., IEEE JESTPE (2021, 72 citations)
"Real-Life Mission Profile-Oriented Lifetime Estimation of SiC HV DC/DC Converter for EV"
- Uses real EV measurement data (244 mission profiles categorized into 3 types)
- Demonstrates proper methodology for lifetime estimation

SOURCE 18: LinkedIn/Impedyme (2025) "Why passive R-L loads fail in inverter testing"
- RL loads cannot capture back-EMF effects
- Motor emulator needed for realistic torque/speed simulation
- Regenerative braking cannot be tested with RL load
- This is a KNOWN LIMITATION of our simulation approach

CRITICAL PEER REVIEW FINDINGS SUMMARY:
=========================================

CATEGORY A - PHYSICS/MODELLING ERRORS:
1. Load model: Pure RL ignores back-EMF → current magnitude is WRONG at high speed
   - At 120km/h, fe=174Hz, back-EMF ≈ 0.8*Vdc/2 = 320V
   - Actual current would be much lower than RL prediction
   - Our sim predicts Ipk = m*Vdc/Z(fe) = 0.9*400/sqrt(1.3²+(2π*174*0.002)²) ≈ 163A
   - With back-EMF: I ≈ (V_applied - V_bemf) / Z ≈ much lower at high speed
   
2. No field weakening region modelled (critical above base speed)

3. FC pre-charging not addressed (industry requirement per Charged EVs)

CATEGORY B - MISSING INDUSTRY REQUIREMENTS:
4. No DC-link capacitor design (ripple current, ESR, lifetime)
5. No EMI filter design (CISPR 25 compliance)
6. No gate driver selection (isolated, with DESAT protection)
7. No active discharge circuit (UN ECE R100 safety requirement)
8. No functional safety analysis (ISO 26262 ASIL)
9. No power density calculation (DOE target: 100kW/L)
10. No busbar design / parasitic inductance analysis

CATEGORY C - COMPONENT SELECTION GAPS:
11. SiC module: generic "450A class" - need specific part number
12. FC capacitor: 1.5mF at 400V - need specific film capacitor selection with 
    ripple current verification
13. No DC-link capacitor specified
14. No gate driver IC specified
15. No current/voltage sensor specified

CATEGORY D - SIMULATION METHODOLOGY:
16. Fixed modulation index (m=0.9) - should vary with speed/torque
17. No closed-loop current control (open-loop V/f only)
18. No overmodulation or six-step operation at high speed
