FC vs NPC TOPOLOGY COMPARISON NOTES (for IEEE paper)
=====================================================

SOURCE: Infineon Community Knowledge Base (Sept 2025)
"Comparative analysis of 3-level topologies"

TABLE: Comparative analysis of 3-level topologies
| Criteria            | NPC                        | T-Type                    | ANPC                      | FC                           |
|---------------------|----------------------------|---------------------------|---------------------------|------------------------------|
| Component Count     | Moderate (diodes+switches) | Low (fewer switches)      | High (active switches)    | High (capacitors+switches)   |
| Voltage Stress      | Low (Vdc/2)                | Moderate (Vdc/2 for some) | Low (Vdc/2)               | Low (Vdc/2)                  |
| Efficiency          | Moderate (diode losses)    | High (fewer components)   | Moderate (switching loss) | Moderate (capacitor losses)  |
| NP Balancing        | Challenging                | Moderate                  | Good (active control)     | Good (natural balancing)     |
| Cost                | Moderate                   | Low                       | High                      | High (capacitors)            |
| Applications        | Medium-voltage drives,grids| Low-medium voltage        | High-power, fault-tolerant| Medium-high voltage          |
| Scalability         | Limited (diode count)      | Limited                   | Moderate                  | Good (more capacitors)       |
| Complexity          | Moderate (control)         | Low                       | High (control)            | High (capacitor balancing)   |

KEY FINDINGS:
- FC: "preferred for applications that require scalability and low harmonic distortion"
- FC: "modular design and ability to minimize harmonic distortion"
- NPC: "well-suited for medium-voltage applications where balancing cost and efficiency is crucial"
- NPC: neutral point balancing is "Challenging" vs FC's "Good (natural balancing)"

SOURCE: EPJ Web of Conferences 330, 01005 (2025) - CISTEM 2024
"Choice between three-level NPC and FC topology under SVM control"
- Both topologies commonly used with single voltage source
- FC uses voltaic condensates for intermediate voltages
- FC offers better adaptation to load variations
- NPC generates very high-quality output voltage
- NPC: capacitor balance management problematic for >3 levels

SOURCE: Charged EVs (2023) "A Closer Look at Multilevel Traction Inverters"
Key points for EV traction:
- FC advantage: No clamping diodes needed (fewer components in conduction path)
- FC advantage: All switches rated at same voltage (Vdc/2) - symmetric thermal stress
- FC advantage: Redundant switching states enable natural voltage balancing
- FC advantage: Modular - can scale to more levels without topology change
- NPC disadvantage: Unequal loss distribution (outer switches carry more loss)
- NPC disadvantage: Clamping diodes add conduction loss and are not controllable
- NPC disadvantage: DC-link midpoint balancing requires active control
- FC disadvantage: Large capacitors add volume, weight, and cost
- FC disadvantage: Pre-charging circuit required at startup
- FC disadvantage: Capacitor reliability (lifetime, ESR degradation)

SOURCE: ETH Zurich (Schweizer et al.) - ECPE Workshop
"Comparative Evaluation of Advanced 3-level Inverter/Converter Topologies"
- At high switching frequencies (>10kHz), FC becomes more attractive because:
  - Capacitor ripple current decreases with higher fsw
  - Smaller capacitors can be used
  - SiC enables high fsw operation

FOR EV TRACTION SPECIFICALLY:
=============================
WHY FC IS CHOSEN OVER NPC:
1. Equal voltage stress on ALL switches → same device can be used everywhere
   (NPC has inner/outer switch asymmetry)
2. Natural capacitor balancing via redundant states → simpler control
   (NPC requires active DC midpoint balancing)
3. No clamping diodes → fewer components in conduction path → lower losses
4. Better fault tolerance → if one capacitor fails, degraded operation possible
5. Scalability to higher levels without topology change
6. Symmetric thermal loading → all switches see similar loss → uniform aging

WHY NPC MIGHT BE PREFERRED:
1. No bulky flying capacitors → smaller volume
2. Lower cost (no expensive film capacitors)
3. More mature technology in industrial drives
4. Simpler for 3-level (complexity increases for FC at higher levels)

FOR 800V EV CONTEXT:
- 800V bus → each switch blocks 400V → 650V devices could work, but 1200V SiC 
  gives margin for transients
- FC natural balancing is critical because EV drive cycles have rapidly changing 
  load conditions → NPC midpoint would drift
- FC capacitor volume is acceptable in EV (not space-constrained like aerospace)
- Pre-charging is already needed for DC-link capacitor in EV → adding FC pre-charge
  is minimal additional complexity

COMPONENT SELECTION RATIONALE:
==============================
SWITCH SELECTION (CAB450M12XM3):
- 1200V rating: Provides 50% voltage margin over 800V bus (400V per switch)
  - Industry standard: 2.5x blocking voltage margin for transient spikes
  - 1200V SiC >> 650V GaN for this application (GaN limited to ~650V)
- 450A rating: Provides 3.3x margin over 136A peak current
  - Accounts for: transient overload, derating at temperature, SOA margin
  - Half-bridge module: integrates 2 switches → only 2 modules per phase leg
- SiC over Si IGBT: 
  - 10x lower switching energy at same current
  - No tail current → clean turn-off
  - Enables high fsw (20kHz) without excessive loss
  - Temperature-independent switching energy (unlike IGBT)
- Package: 62mm industry-standard footprint
  - Compatible with existing liquid cooling plates
  - Low stray inductance (8nH) for clean switching

CAPACITOR SELECTION (680µF):
- Sizing equation: C = Ipk / (2 * fsw_min * ΔVfc)
  - Ipk = 136A (worst case urban)
  - fsw_min = 5 kHz (lowest scheduled frequency)
  - ΔVfc = 20V (5% of 400V reference)
  - C = 136 / (2 * 5000 * 20) = 680 µF
- Film capacitor (not electrolytic):
  - High ripple current capability (>100A RMS)
  - Low ESR (<5mΩ) → minimal self-heating
  - Long lifetime (>100,000 hours at 85°C)
  - Self-healing capability
  - No polarity constraint
- Candidate parts: TDK EPCOS B32778 series, KEMET C4AQ series
  - Both rated for automotive (AEC-Q200 qualified)
  - 450V DC rating with 20% derating at 400V
  - Operating temperature: -40°C to +105°C
